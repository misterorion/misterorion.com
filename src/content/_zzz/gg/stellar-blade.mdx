## Seasons

On January 1, 2025, I decided to get my CISSP certification and spent the next seven months studying intensely. During the last of those months, July, leading up to the exam date, I took a week off of work to do review my notes, organize my test-taking strategy, and force some downtime with some gaming. I locked in: 6 hours of review in the morning and early afternoon, followed by a two-mile walk, and then some _Stellar Blade_.

This game appeared on my radar when it released on PS4 in 2024, having generated a bit of controversy surrounding the character design. The sexuality of the character design generated the predictable negative reaction and criticism from the usual tranches of the media. But it looked fun, nonetheless.

When the crossover between _Stellar Blade_ and _NieR: Automata_ was announced, I began following it more closely.

It turned out the game is heavily inspired by _NieR_, almost to a fault. A term I learned that describes both games so well is The feeling of "Dystopian melancholy" main characters are androids (Andro-Eidos), the setting is a post-apocalyptic Earth with an orbital space station, the soundtrack features etherial vocal tracks, questioning the nature of humanity. "Dystopian melancholy" (records, vending machines). The floating pods/drones. Eve and 2B are both skilled soldiers sent from an orbital space station to reclaim Earth for humanity. I recently learned that a few tracks were composed by MONICA the studio founded by _NieR_ composer Keiichi Okabe. Emil roming around the desert, the fishing. I guess you could describe it as a love letter.

## Controversy

But that didn't stop it from becoming a huge success. This was a triumph, or a swing in the other direction from the "woke" mind-virus that had been plaguing the entertainment industry for a few years.

When the game was finally released on Steam, it was a day-one purchase. If you look at the recent discussions on Steam community, they all express appreciation for giving gamers what they want, and ignoring the journalists and activists. A BREATH OF FRESH AIR, FINALLY.

There were quite a few aspects that reminded me of one of my all-time favorite games, _Nier: Automata_. The open-world and music especially. No doubt this was intentional, if you recall the crossovers with _Nier_ in-game (the roaming character).

I'm going to go back and play this some more over the coming months.

The collectables were fun to find. Fishing was a bit of a chore, but the rewards were worth it.

It does seem like the future is bright for games like this.
